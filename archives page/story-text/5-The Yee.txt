1. The Yee Section
An oral history told by Harry, Simon, Clay, Jimmy and Kenzo.
The Origins
Simon: Maybe we’re just an entity. We’re a unit, an army, a force of creativity. The best way to put it is if you think about how The Roots play behind Erykah Badu or D’Angelo, that’s kind of what we are for the Bay. A collective of talented musicians and human beings who are all really good at their craft.
Kenzo: The Yee Section is a collective, a band of individuals from the Bay Area who came together around LaRussell’s movement. We started as the band, but it grew into something bigger. We just did our first show today, and I’m excited for what’s next. I feel like I came in at the perfect time, like it was meant to happen that way.
Harry: Yeah, it’s just a group of musicians who all kind of met through LaRussell. We grew up in the Bay Area, so that’s where the connection really started. There’s this shared rhythm, that Bay swing, it’s something you feel more than you read off paper.
Clay: The name actually came from that. We were talking about how a lot of beats are just eight bars looping, and we’d add a new section, switch it up. So we started saying, “Yeah, you got the A section, but do you got the Yee Section?”
Jimmy: I joined before it was even called that. It happened organically, just showing up at the pergola with my flute and getting thrown into the mix. I’ve known Simon for years, we’d played in a lot of bands before this, so when it started forming, it felt natural.
The Sound
Simon: Everything we do is on command, played by ear. We never know the set list, and that’s the magic. If we don’t know what’s coming next, neither does the crowd. It’s like scoring something live, in real time.
Kenzo: I still feel like I’m finding my way, because when you’re a DJ, you’re used to controlling everything. But in a band, it’s different. You’re not leading, you’re assisting. You’re filling in, seeing where you fit, and sometimes you don’t. It’s been humbling and really fun to learn that balance.
Harry: A lot of what makes it different is that we weren’t practicing for a long time. Sometimes we’d be on stage hearing a song for the first time and just have to pick it up. It’s all improvisation, each show becomes a new experience.
Clay: It’s a lot of improv, a lot of learning songs on the spot. I’ve grown so much as a piano player because of it. When you’re surrounded by people who listen and leave their egos out of it, it’s just fun. No one’s tripping, no one’s mad, everyone’s easy to be around.
Jimmy: What makes it different is the musicianship. We don’t rehearse much, but everyone shows up prepared. The music stays authentic and unplanned, it just happens organically, and that’s what keeps it alive.
The Descriptors
Kayla: I want to go down the line, say a word or animal that comes to mind for each member.
Harry: Michael, visionary. Geo, bubbly. Clay, suave. Jimmy, mysterious. Simon, maestro.
Clay: Geo’s a puppy. Michael’s like a lizard. Harry’s an ostrich. Jimmy’s a flamingo. Kenzo’s a lion.
Kenzo: Harry, fantastic. Geo, rare. Jimmy, extraordinary. Clay, calm. Simon, genius. Michael, brain. Everyone in the Yee Section has something unique about them.
Jimmy: Geo’s a butterfly. Simon’s a chameleon, he can adapt to anything. Kenzo’s a silverback, strong leader energy. Clay’s a kangaroo, just bouncing into any zone to make music. Harry’s a giraffe, tall and funky. Michael’s some kind of aquatic animal, maybe a dolphin.
Simon: Harry’s a Bengal tiger. Kenzo’s a hyena, always laughing. Geo’s a swan. Jimmy’s an owl. Clay’s a peacock, colorful as hell.
The Collective
Clay: We’re all on similar waves. Everyone’s hungry for this music thing, and there’s no ego. It’s like Simon’s the MD, so we listen to him, but it’s not about control, it’s about trust.
Kenzo: I feel like we’re all patient and kind people, and that translates into how we treat the music. When you’re patient and kind with art, it fills you up. It teaches you to step back, to know when to add and when to let others shine.
Simon: What makes a great musician isn’t how much they play, it’s knowing when not to play. When you’ve got ten people on stage, you have to make space for each other. Sometimes I’ll stop playing entirely just so everyone else can shine. That’s what makes us a living organism.
Jimmy: It’s really like a family. We check in with each other outside of music, and that energy shows up on stage. The chemistry comes from actually caring.
The Moments
Kayla: What’s your favorite Yee Section or GC memory?
Harry: Outside Lands. Being backstage, getting free massages, just taking it all in. That was surreal.
Simon: Tiny Desk. Only one percent of artists get to do that, and to do it with your friends, that’s something I’ll never forget. After Outside Lands, we were celebrating in the green room, and Ludacris just walked by. Like, what is life?
Clay: I remember the night before Tiny Desk, when we cooked up a new song in the Airbnb. Everyone was there. It’s those little moments, the creative ones that happen by accident, that I remember most.
Kenzo: When we made “Do You Wanna Be My Best Friend,” that was a really special moment. Even though not everyone was there, it felt like a breakthrough. And watching Tiny Desk from home, I still felt connected, like I was right there.
Jimmy: For me, it’s showing up at the pergola before the band even had a name. That’s where it all started.
The Group Chat
Simon: “Anybody free to do a live session tomorrow?” Either no one replies, or we just send memes.
Jimmy: A lot of trolling. A lot of stickers. But also planning, like where we gotta be, what time. It’s a mix of business and jokes. That’s what keeps it fun.
Kenzo: We organize live sessions, talk about future shows, send motivational texts. Sometimes I’ll finish a five-mile run and drop something encouraging in there. Sometimes we just talk nonsense, but it’s love every time.
Looking Ahead
Harry: I think we’ll all keep thriving individually, but that’ll open doors for the Yee Section to grow. Maybe like how Anderson Paak’s band became its own thing.
Clay: Right now we’re all balancing other projects, but I could see us getting booked on our own one day. Maybe when things slow down a little, we’ll build something bigger.
Kenzo: I live in the now, but if I had to look ahead, I see Grammys, headline shows, stadium tours. We’ve got something special, and the world will see that soon.
Jimmy: Five or ten years from now, I see us as one of the greatest to come out of the Bay. Not just a band from here, but a band that travels, collaborates, and keeps pushing what live music can be.
Simon: We’re still at the beginning. The Yee Section isn’t done becoming whatever it’s meant to be.
