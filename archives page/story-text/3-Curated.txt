Story 5: Curated 4 Selfworth
In conversation with Hokage Simon
Simon crafted a slow-moving love letter to himself with Curated 4 Selfworth, which debuted on streaming platforms in mid-August. The ten-track album holds your hand through loss, love, healing, resilience, and creative passion. Collaborations with Simon’s equally talented friends embellish the project, including Michael Sneed’s distinctive high-pitched flows, which capture a multi-generational love story marked by grief and immense growth; Shante’s one-of-a-kind vocals; and Seiji’s meditative letter to self. Contributions from P-Lo, Nate Curry, and Tommy Jumpman round out the album, giving it the dream team feel—like the U.S. Basketball Team at the 2024 Paris Olympics.
The album showcases Simon’s range—from singing to producing to masterminding unforgettable collaborations. While Simon’s name often appears in credits across the board (LaRussell, Thuy, P-Lo, E-40, Karri, Larry June, Rexx Life Raj, Elujay, Full Crate, G-Eazy, Christian Kuria, Serpent With Feet, and many more), it’s rare to see him release his own music. This project was a long time coming.
“This was, like, one of the hardest projects for me to put out because I was holding on to everything for way too long,” Simon admitted.
He added, “It makes me feel good too, because now that this old music is out, I get to put my mindset on making new music. You know, it’s like cleaning out your closet—when you want to get new clothes, you’ve got to take the old clothes out first.”
Take Changes, for example. The track, created four years ago, finally came to life and P-Lo jumped on it—once Simon convinced himself to release it. “I’m blessed enough to work with so many talented people, and I’m really just honored to play a part in so many artists’ creative worlds. So when it comes to me trying to water my own plants and build my own world, and people actually want to be a part of it... it makes me feel really great.”
I haven’t met an artist more consistent or highly spoken of than Simon. He’s effortlessly cool, endlessly humble, and kind to everyone. Simon has an unmatched ear for music and I’ve never seen him without his art. Every plane ride, car trip, pre-show moment, or post-gig downtime—Simon was creating. Whether it was his keyboard, laptop, or headphones, living and creating ran in one vein for him.
This commitment made Simon a go-to collaborator, always ready to say yes to close friends. That’s why this album is so special—it’s a moment for him to shine on his own. Simon started making beats in high school, though he didn’t have many artists to work with back then. Around 20 or 21, he began immersing himself in the Vallejo music scene, where his name first started spreading.
I discovered Simon’s work during COVID when P-Lo dropped STUNNA. Since then, I’ve slowly uncovered Simon’s endless G.O.A.T. lore. People would say things like, “Simon was traveling the world when he was 5!” or “He played in bars with bands as a teen!” The truth? He’s been playing in bands since middle school. His dad, filled with pride, would tell anyone who’d listen: “My son can play keys, bro.” That pride landed Simon gigs with Vallejo group CRSB, performing in clubs and touring while still in high school.
“Performing, especially with Russ, is like second nature because I’ve been doing this for so long,” Simon shared.
And it showed. Simon played a pivotal role in shaping Good Compenny’s iconic concert structure. Before 2022, it was mainly LaRussell and Simon doing shows. Slowly, new layers were added—Michael Prince with the violin during the 2022 Pergola show, Richy Cruz on trumpet, and dozens of musicians over the last two years. Through every era, Simon’s foundation has been instrumental.
“Simon, Hokage Simon, is one of my inspirations. He’s one of the most incredible musicians I’ve ever met,” said Harry (MadeByHarry), who joined Good Compenny at the end of 2023. “He just picks up the song instantly, playing chords, creating bass lines and melodies. It’s my job to fit in where I can.”
ClayDough added, “He’s a goat. So kind, so nice, and he works so hard. He doesn’t gatekeep, either. He’s given me so much game in production, piano playing, and live performance. Gotta give it up to Simon.”
At one point, someone said: “Nobody can outplay Simon.”
Simon has spent years building a community of like-minded musicians, working with close friends to create something beautiful. Slowly but surely, more people began recognizing his name. The same lore I heard from community has proven true—Simon’s story is one of passion, persistence, and unmatched artistry.
And now, with Curated 4 Self Worth, the world finally gets to hear Simon on his own terms.
