8. All Star Weekend 2025: Splitting the Pie, a Recipe That Never Fails
All-Star Weekend not only celebrated the legacy of basketball, but also its intersection with fashion, entertainment, music, and most importantly, the Bay Area. Before the weekend kicked off, Tietta sent out a meticulously crafted schedule that spanned from Wednesday to Sunday. The schedule itself took nearly ten hours to create, after revisions, without factoring in the time spent planning the actual event. It’s hard to grasp the sheer number of hours that go into producing an event like All-Star Weekend. Across the Bay Area, there were hundreds of installations, ranging from major sports brands to podcasts, and parties sponsored by nearly every brand under the sun. There was an undeniable sense of excitement leading into the weekend, but also a tinge of anxiety. With such a packed schedule, one mistake or delay could impact the rest of the events.
LaRussell is known for “splitting the pie.” He’s just as recognized for being an individual as he is for leading a team that stands out among the rest. It’s the professionalism, efficiency, and high standards upheld by Good Compenny that earned them the reputation. Maintaining that reputation required constant effort, and it was something none of us forgot, especially that weekend. With so many opportunities packed into the event, LaRussell had the chance to bring more people than ever to experience it. All-Star Weekend was about ensuring that everyone had a chance to shine, both on and off the court.
FCTRY LAB
All-Star Weekend kicked off with a business meeting in San Francisco to connect with the owners of FCTRY LAB. Good Compenny arrived in a sprinter van at an apartment near the Embarcadero. As we all piled in through the doors, the owner of the house remarked that he wasn’t expecting this many people. Everyone found a spot, whether sprawled out on the floor, sitting on a chair, or securing a lucky spot on the couch.
FCTRY LAB had worked with Kanye and YEEZY in the past, and more recently with NLE Choppa on his duck boot. LaRussell loved the idea of creating his first shoe, one that everyone would recognize and that could become a staple. They previewed the shoe during a slideshow: a foam shoe in several colorways, which the whole team loved. But once the first few price points were thrown out, the conversation took a turn.
“If there are four people in their family, that’ll cost almost a thousand dollars.” The quick math made LaRussell realize that an average family from Vallejo, who grew up like him, wouldn’t be able to afford it.
LaRussell went back and forth, exchanging ideas with FCTRY LAB, who traditionally made more expensive items, to come up with something that was both accessible and cool. LaRussell continued to emphasize that he wasn’t focused on short-term profit and loss. He was willing to invest more capital upfront if it helped make the project work on their end. The main goal was for every kid who looked up to him to be able to afford to look like him. This was one of LaRussell’s superpowers, he was able to think quickly in the short term and take action, while always keeping a long-term vision in mind. After a brief debate, both teams agreed that they could make something work. What that looks like, you’ll have to stay tuned to find out.
Rich Paul Panel With Klutch & E.L.I.T.E. Students
Next, we headed over to Union Square Shoe Palace for a panel conversation with Rich Paul, creator of the brand Klutch and sports agent to some of the biggest names in entertainment, including LeBron James, Anthony Davis, and Draymond Green. But instead of the usual room filled with influencers and sports fans, the space was packed with high school students from E.L.I.T.E Public Schools, the school based in Vallejo that LaRussell’s daughter also attended. The students were all gifted clothes with the signature black and yellow Klutch colorway and had been invited by LaRussell for a chance to soak up some free game from both him and Rich Paul. When asked about LaRussell’s invitation, Rich Paul said it was a no-brainer, when LaRussell wanted to bring the young people with him. The students took full advantage of the opportunity, asking questions about navigating life after graduation, Rich Paul’s unconventional career path, and hearing insights from leading sports journalist Malika Andrews, who is from Oakland. LaRussell reminded the group that when he was their age, opportunities like this simply didn’t exist.
NBA All Star Rising Game
The next stop on his NBA All-Star Game tour was the All-Star Rising Stars Game, where LaRussell didn’t just bring Simon and Michael to perform, he also brought a full lineup of dancers from across the country, including Diva, June, Storm DeBarge, Hurricane, and Lex. The 60-second performance highlighted national talent, bringing hip-hop culture from the Bay Area, the South, and the East Coast, all while staying rooted in the Bay Area.
Marshawn Lynch x Jordan Jam Event in Alameda
The trend of splitting the pie continued with the Marshawn Lynch x Jordan event, held at the typically empty Alameda hangars. A massive Jordan installation was built, with a huge stage in the middle and light beams coming down from every angle. The Jordan Jam event reminded me of Kendrick Lamar’s pop-out, and the lineup was absolutely stacked. The preshow included a dance battle featuring the Turf Feinz, and the main event had a once-in-a-lifetime lineup, including LaRussell, Kehlani, Mistah F.A.B., Larry June, 2Chainz, Seiji Oda, Quavo and more. The crowd was by far the most star-studded event we attended. It was a mix of athletes, influencers, rappers, Bay Area locals and singers. It was a moment to showcase Bay Area talent to a crowd that likely wouldn’t have made it out to the Bay otherwise.
LaRussell didn’t just perform; he brought the full Yee Section, made up of Jimmy, Simon, Claydough, Tione, Michael, Geo, and Harry, who played not only for him but for most of the show, backing a range of artists. LaRussell also took control of the show, at Marshawn’s request, orchestrating artists as they transitioned on and off the stage.
That’s one thing I’ve always admired about LaRussell, his unwavering standard for the way he performed. It often came down to the smallest details that were glaringly obvious to him and the instrumentalists but largely unnoticed by the crowd. Despite this, LaRussell was meticulous with the sound and the run of the show. Instead of focusing only on his own set or his team, he made sure those standards were applied to the entire event, from start to finish. It was another way LaRussell ensured no one was left behind.
There was a moment during the show when I looked to my right and saw the long stage that allowed the Yee Section to comfortably perform, something rare, as the big ensemble typically had to fit in wherever they could. On stage across from them was Mistah F.A.B., Seiji Oda, Michael Sneed, LaRussell, Tietta, Jason, Marshawn Lynch, and many more. It was a surreal moment to witness the talent, past, present, and future, being captured, all at a magnitude we rarely saw.
This came at a particularly important time for the Bay Area. Too often, the region has been faced with stigma from outsiders. The media frequently paints the Bay Area as run-down, dangerous, and practically unlivable. When I tell people where I’m from, their faces shift, they grimace, offer an apology, and reference the city’s “current state.” I find myself caught between defending my home and acknowledging the complexities of the truth. But for those of us who call the Bay home, there’s also a collective hope, that this moment wouldn’t reinforce the negative narratives that have long overshadowed our city. And the weekend delivered.
Steph Curry Court of Gold Brunch
To close out the week, LaRussell headlined at Steph Curry’s Court of Gold event at Club Thirty at the Chase Center. He brought along The Yee Section, as well as the CO-LLAB Choir, a powerhouse collective of singers based in Oakland.
LaRussell barely took up any time performing his own music during his set. Instead, he spent the time celebrating the other artists in attendance. He orchestrated an impromptu performance, pulling up Money B from Digital Underground to perform “I Get Around,” followed by P-Lo for Put Me On Somethin’, then Mistah F.A.B. for New Oakland, Richie Rich for I Got 5 on It, Saweetie for Tap In, and finally, the peak: Too Short stepping up for Blow the Whistle.
Steph Curry and Matt Barnes were posted on the wall, screaming Too Short’s infamous “Biiiitch!” ad-libs. The crowd of mainly industry folk were filled with thizz faces, the energy hyphy asf and very Bay Area. The kids were dancing, the whole stage was moving and it was another example of LaRussell’s mastery that showcased the lineage of hip-hop.
The effort it took to coordinate this weekend was unfathomable. Tietta’s attention to detail and planning skills were more important than ever and truly reflected her greatness. LaRussell’s work ethic also shone through. While it’s not something many question, it’s hard to comprehend unless you’re in his position, one very few people occupy. Mornings started at 6 AM and ended at 4 AM. While artists and people like myself could come later, leave earlier, or sneak off for lunch, LaRussell not only had to remain present, but constantly performed with a beaming smile, ready to interact with eager fans. Along the way, he also maintained a relentless pursuit of bringing in every person possible. That’s the true superpower.
