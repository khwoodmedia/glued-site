11. DJ Day$: Crowd Control
After a ten-hour flight from SFO, DJDay$ landed in London in the thick of July’s heat. Everything felt unfamiliar, from the accents to cars on the wrong side of the road. It was only his second time abroad, the first being a brief trip to Canada just weeks before. He and Karri were in the middle of touring with Isaiah Falls when they took a quick detour to open for Drake in London.
Still exhausted from the flight, he landed already in motion, preparing for his DJ set while simultaneously stepping into the role of impromptu tour manager, coordinating between Drake’s team and Karri for Drake’s 2025 Wireless Festival performance at Finsbury Park, a 50,000-capacity venue.
The feeling was indescribable, and yet it was only the beginning.
Grayson, better known as DJDay$, had been manifesting this life since eighth grade, when he quietly added “@4karri’s DJ” to his Instagram bio. It began when he transferred to a new middle school in San Francisco. Shy and unsure of himself, he made an early mistake, calling Karri trash at basketball. Karri gave him the cold shoulder for a week, but after patching things over, the two became inseparable. Today, they’ve moved through every era together, from middle school, to early music experiments to releasing albums, and playing sold-out shows.
Music had been a throughline for Day$ long before sold out crowds. In eighth grade, he was just a kid who loved the radio and supporting his friend’s music. By freshman year of high school, he joined the DJ Club, though he was too nervous to actually spin in front of people and share his work. But everything shifted after Rolling Loud. Watching Travis Scott, Playboi Carti, and his favorite artists move entire crowds made something click. I want to do something like that, he thought. He quickly realized he couldn’t rap, but every artist had a DJ. That was his in and he was ready to take it more seriously.
In sophomore year, a haircut changed everything. His cousin Zmo—also a DJ—turned the appointment into a crash course in how to get started DJ’ing. When he returned home, Day$ pulled out his iPad and downloaded every app Zmo mentioned. He began experimenting. He started producing on Ableton, creating drum loops and tweaking BPMs until something felt right. Then when school went online, he finally got a laptop, saved for his first mixer, and spent COVID teaching himself to DJ from scratch.
By the time he returned to school as a senior, the DJ Club had fallen apart, many of the seniors and juniors leaving after COVID. But he still showed up faithfully. Slowly he built the courage to share mixes online. SoundCloud, Instagram, Snapchat—bit by bit, he let the world hear the ear he’d always had.
By senior year, when he applied to USC, he imagined a double life: journalist by day, DJ by night. He’d worked on his high school’s newspaper and assumed that would be his “real job,” with DJ’ing as a side gig. Batman, but with a press badge. But then the breaks started coming.
The summer of 2022, before Grayson left for USC, a friend asked him to open up for an artist at Brick and Mortar. His sound, hip-hop, trap, underground rap, rage music, made people move. The crowd loved him. Confidence began to bloom. For the first time, he wondered if DJ’ing wasn’t just an option, but option A.
Freshman year at USC further solidified his instinct. He was out every weekend, not because he chased nightlife, but because DJ’ing placed him at the center of it. It helped him meet people, connected him to businesses, and built his name. He DJ’d his first frat party on Halloween. Suddenly, everyone wanted “their DJ,” and for the freshman class, he became that person.
Soon, the stages got bigger: opening for Baby Smoove, D Savage, Wifisfuneral. Then, performing for crowds of 1,500. He watched as the room jumped under his command.
He loved how people found comfort in an album and used concerts to alchemize their energy. DJ’ing wasn’t just a job; it was a service, a way of giving people a place to feel good.
“I just wanted to put a smile on people’s faces,” he said. “I want people to leave a show with a good memory. That’s the whole point.”
He continued to grow at USC, DJ’ing more parties, at a bigger scale. At the same time, his middle school best friend Karri, was charting his own artistic path.
But as senior year of college approached, pressure crept in. Friends, artists he admired, were hanging up their dreams for careers. He felt the pull of “real jobs,” and the urgency of adulthood calling him. Still, something in him resisted the idea of stepping away. Instead, he leaned in harder: planning events, supporting Karri with artist management, eager to uplift others in the music industry. He never wanted to look back and wonder if he could have made it if he’d just kept going.
“I’m always trying to keep that hunger,” he said. “That hunger is what drives the best work.”
Through everything, he stayed grounded. He recalled a time, back in 2019, when he and Karri worked a funnel cake stand at Outside Lands, slicing strawberries while P-Lo performed onstage just yards away. They laughed about how badly they wished they could watch the show.
“We have the same energy, just a bigger stage,” said Day$.
This was the payoff of years of showing up, practicing alone, spinning at lunch, posting mixes when he was scared, and claiming “@4karri’s DJ” before he was even ready.
And he’s not done.
What started with an iPad and a haircut has taken him across the world. And somehow, it still feels like he’s just getting started.
