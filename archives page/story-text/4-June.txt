6. June
Back in the Bay, June is planting roots and building the future he’s been dreaming of, right at home.
June grew up in Richmond, California, spending much of his childhood immersed in basketball and enjoying the quiet comfort of solitude. Naturally shy, he poured most of his energy into sports. In seventh grade, he made his first appearance in a film. His older brother had developed a passion for YouTube skits and acting, transforming their dad’s Afrocentric store in Oakland, filled with incense, African art, candles, and other cultural treasures, into a makeshift set. June, not one for the spotlight, agreed to be an extra, sitting in the background with a newspaper covering his face as the camera focused on his brother.
Throughout middle and high school, June stuck to a strict routine: school, practice, homework, sleep, repeat. But as senior year approached, he realized basketball couldn’t be the only path forward, so he began exploring new interests. At Berkeley High, he’d always enjoyed drawing and being creative, though he wasn’t yet sure where it would lead.
After graduation, June took the energy and discipline he had developed through basketball and funneled it into dance. He studied videos, recorded and critiqued himself, then tried again. Soon, he was sharing videos on Snapchat, and his friends encouraged him to keep going.
That period also marked a turning point. He began stepping out of his comfort zone, attending parties, performing in front of larger crowds, and engaging more socially. He even started the “Tiny Glasses Gang,” where he’d pass around tiny frames at parties, and whoever wore them had to dance. It was June’s way of bringing something different to the party.
During that time, he met a photographer who taught him the basics of content creation, filming skits, incorporating dance, building narratives, and scheduling posts. He jumped on dance trends like the Milly Rock and Harlem Shake, broadening his reach. About a year into consistent filming, June posted a skit from the UC Berkeley parking garage about being from the Bay but wishing he were in New York. He went to bed with 2,000 followers and woke up with 11,000. Complex shared the video, celebrities began reaching out, and June realized this could be more than just a hobby.
One of his most viral moments came with the creation of the “June Bug Challenge.” The video took off, with some of his favorite celebrities joining in. Brand partnerships followed, and suddenly, content creation became a full-time career, even before there was a clear name for it.
Now with over 600K followers, June reflects on how far he’s come. Seventh-grade June couldn’t have imagined building a whole career around being on camera and performing on some of the biggest stages in the world. Most recently, he performed with LaRussell and a group of dancers at the NBA All-Star Rising Stars Game in the Bay Area. Basketball was his first love, he never imagined he’d still end up on the court, just in a different way.
Earlier in the year, June made the decision to move back to the Bay. After nearly a decade of creating content, he was ready to think about what’s next. LaRussell’s work in Vallejo inspired him to consider how he could give back to his own city.
“He’s doing things for his community in Vallejo, and I look at that and think, okay, what can I do for my city in Richmond?” said June.
Last summer, LaRussell invited June to the V to shoot some visualizers. The collaboration came naturally, and it reminded June of what’s possible when you create from home. Tired of being mistaken for being from LA or New York, June felt called to come home to the Bay.
Now, he’s back in the Bay and focused on what’s next. He’s interested in directing, knows he’s a great marketer, and wants to spend more time at his local community center. He’s also committed to building alongside Good Compenny, staying grounded in the place that raised him.
