Story 1: Sam Opoku
I'm sitting with Sam Opoku at Create Studios in Los Angeles, California in August. The singer and songwriter has just wrapped up his first session with LaRussell, and we're here to talk about his time in the booth, his music, and his background. Originally from West Ghana and now based in Phoenix, Arizona, Opoku first connected with Good Compenny for a Live Session. Since then, he’s performed with Shante in his hometown of Phoenix. Now, he’s in the studio recording with LaRussell, bringing a blend of soulful vocals to complement LaRussell’s ever evolving sound.
Sam says he aims to walk the line between Afrobeats and Frank Ocean, crafting a sound that’s unmistakably his own. His voice, both silky and emotive, instantly moves you.
What did you think when you first heard LaRussell & his music?
Just how dope and fresh and authentic it was. Yeah, it just feels, it feels like him all the way through. So it was something that it reminded me of, something that me and my team want to do, just go out and shoot what feels natural, what he always says is like his life is his content, and it comes through in the music as well. So it’s definitely palpable from top to bottom, and it’s inspiring to say the least.
And this is the first time y’all have hopped in the booth together?
Yes this is the first time, this is the first booth hopping. Yeah.
What is it like to actually be in the energy and the space to create together?
It's dope. It's like because I've experienced it online, experience at the compound. But when it’s like music making time, it’s a whole different energy, because now you're getting to actually create and put it down and watching the process is dope, too. Everybody has their own unique, takes and habits and stuff. So to be in that like, guy, let’s go, let’s move the machine. Energy is, like, It’s infectious. So, yeah, it is super, super dope. It’s inspiring.
Can you tell us a little bit about where you're from and what were your early musical influences, what you were listening to in the house?
I'm from Ghana, West Africa, and I came to the States when I was like eight years old, and I grew up in, a singer environment. But that’s just how our culture is. We have a song for everything. When we're to church or coming back from church and we're about to sleep or about to eat, when you're about to go use the restroom, you got a song for it, you know? So it just naturally fills up the house. A lot of stuff that I was listening to is like hymns and like old blues records and stuff that my dad would play.
And there’s a sound out of West Africa called high life, and it’s the origins of, like, Afro beat and Caribbean and that kind of vibe. And so a lot of that infused into what I was doing, along with in school, I was listening to, like, the oldies, like Ella Fitzgerald and even Frank Sinatra. Right now, I really love singer songwriters like the James Blakes of the world and Bon Iver and those type of people, just people who be like writing, writing, you know, that’s where, my heart and soul is. So as far back as you can remember, anybody who's a writer from the Temptations to Lil Wayne, you know, is an inspiration. So I take a lot of pride in the versatility of being able to jump from a hip hop record into like a soulful ballad into an afro beat. I think that’s the lane that I want to ride down. Whatever Frank Ocean would sound on that Afro pop record. That's, that’s the lane I’m trying to go into.

Sam goes on to sing a hymn in his native language, which I, unfortunately, won’t attempt to capture in words.
That was so beautiful. Coming from a culture where it seems like music and life are really intertwined. Do you kind of bring that into your creative process?
That's like every artist's life is meshing the art and the reality of your situations. Yeah, for sure, I’ve had a girl whose favorite color was yellow. I was talking to her for a little bit, and it kind of went south. So I made a song called Yellow. She said her favorite color yellow. And it’s like a therapy session to come to terms with what the color yellow symbolizes. It can be like all these nasty things, or it can be like the sunlight and you know what I’m saying. So bringing those two together really helped as a therapy session. Song called Boomerang of the girl going back and forth to her ex. And those all come from life experiences that only you can write, and it helps you process those moments and get, like a cathartic resolution from so yeah, for sure, life, the imitating art for sure.
What about Good Compenny? Do you really align with or resonate with just as a whole?
I think just the togetherness aspect, like, we're all going to eat together. And so, like, if you don’t have a skill, find a skill. And let’s get you in here thing that’s super dope. A lot of times we look outside for help, but usually it’s right next to us within our own family. So we just got to empower people and build them up to be able to sustain their own piece of the pie. And I feel like that’s something that Good Compenny does really well or emulates well, and it’s something that I want to do with my team as well, you know. So nobody feels like they're being dragged along, but they have, like, a tangible place that they could, like, feed themselves off of.
What can expect from you in the next year or so? What do you got going on?
Yeah, so I’m working on my own EP project. It’s gonna be a four part EP, and we'll tell the story of how I came from Ghana to America, and all the misconceptions I had, finding love, losing love, wanting to go back home, going home, experiencing that. Basically, my life story and a four part EP. So I’m super excited, because I wanted to all connect and be meaningful.
Is there anything I haven’t asked you about that you think we should be paying attention to do?
Yeah, I think you got everything. Just Arizona. Y'all gotta come out to Arizona desert. You’re gonna fry up, but it’s but it’s fire out there. Literally, the people are dope. The music scene is slowly growing, and they need to see more authentic, like artists out there that they can, like, latch on to and support. So, yeah, come out to Phoenix.
