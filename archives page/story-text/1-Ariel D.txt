Story 4: Ariel: Driven by Faith and Fashion
How Ariel Is Transforming LaRussell’s Personal Style and Pushing Him Beyond His Comfort Zone While Staying True to Her Tomboy Roots
Ariel’s “uniform” growing up was the combination of big white t-shirts and jeans, with either a pair of pink and white Shaqs or Nikes and clean, straight-back cornrows.
“I was poor, so we had to get what we get,” Ariel said.
In middle school, Ariel remembers walking around Lake Merritt with her mom, begging for an extra baggy pair of boys’ jeans each time she saw them in a store. As an avid athlete, most of Ariel’s early childhood revolved around basketball. She wore sweats daily and stayed clear of skirts, shorts and tight jeans.
“I was really a boy,” she said.
Ariel didn’t start carving out her style until 9th grade when boys came into the picture. Freshman year also meant the peak of her punk rock phase, full of colorful bangles and belts, which Ariel said was the first and last trend that she ever really tried out.
By sophomore year, Ariel started putting fits together daily. With the help of her first job in high school at McDonalds, she could start buying pieces to craft her style. She became obsessed with vintage items and the joy of a trip to thrift stores. With her finds from Goodwill and The Salvation Army, Ariel would get busy reconstructing pieces to fit her aesthetic, which was far from girly. Ariel remained true to her tomboy affinity for sneakers and comfortable baggy pants, steering clear of skinny jeans, shorts and skirts, for the most part.
“I was wearing ties, with shorts—denim shorts—tights and Timberland boots. Just doin’ stuff,” she said, laughing.
Ariel’s love of experimenting with textures, silhouettes, and unlikely combinations fueled her passion for creating unique looks and growing appetite for fashion. Sometimes, she’d even color her hair to coordinate with her outfits. Eventually, she started getting recognized by her classmates and peers. During her senior year of high school, Ariel won best dressed at Dewey Academy.
While Ariel loved that fashion allowed her to express herself, her priority through middle school, high school and through junior college remained basketball because she wanted to play professionally.
But when Ariel got pregnant with her daughter, Kylie in 2012, Ariel realized she needed to have a plan B. She thought about being a social worker because always loved kids and she considered becoming a dental hygienist, once she started realizing that basketball couldn’t sustain her financially as a new mom without a professional contract. The thought of styling passed her mind but Ariel chopped it up to a dream more than reality. She still loved putting fits together, often helping her friends just for fun.
In 2016 year, Ally Cocaine, a friend and artist, started asking Ariel for help putting together outfits in her early stages of being an artist. Ariel helped Ally out with styling a shoot, still not realizing that her career was forming in front of her eyes. When Ally stepped out in the outfit that Ariel put together, all eyes were on Ally.
“I looked around the room—it was like a movie,” Ariel said.
Creating Movie Moments
Styling Ally Cocaine expanded the boundaries of what Ariel thought was possible, leading her to many opportunities, including becoming LaRussell’s stylist—but not without some bumps along the way. A short stint in Los Angeles in hopes of gaining more experience as a stylist turned sour after Ariel realized that the energy and environment were not for her. While she was there, Ariel quickly hit the goals she’d set out to accomplish as a stylist—working with big names, brands, athletes and rappers—but it became clear to her that Hollywood was too Hollywood. Ariel moved back to the Bay Area, writing off celebrities and rappers forever.
“I told myself I would only work with another artist if I had the opportunity to come up with them,” Ariel said.
Redefining LaRussell’s Style
LaRussell and Ariel connected in late 2023, though they knew about each other’s work for years prior. For Ariel, it was a matter of waiting until she felt prepared. LaRussell wasn’t a “typical” client. He was known and loved for his laid back attire and authentic nature. It’s what made him so relatable to people and what really allowed his talents to speak for themselves. At the same time, Ariel wanted to see LaRussell switch up his everyday attire of Crocs and hoodies.
Ariel saw the opportunity to work with LaRussell as a chance to grow and journey with an artist, rather than be dragged along.
“Wow—I literally manifested this,” Ariel said. “I literally said, ‘I only want to work with somebody who is a good person who I can grow with,’ and that’s literally what I’m doing with the LaRussell right now.”
Ariel’s impact on LaRussell’s image was palatable. Suddenly, LaRussell’s tan Crocs and hoodies became paired with colorful beanies and knit jackets, echoing much of the symbolism that LaRussell spoke about like the work ethic of his favorite athletes and his hometown of Vallejo. Eventually, custom letterman jackets, fuzzy bucket hats and even sunglasses—something LaRussell initially said no to—became staples of his aesthetic.
Ariel spent a long time thinking about what would work for LaRussell’s style, and although she always seemed calm, cool and collected, Ariel couldn’t shake her nerves during her and LaRussell’s first fitting.
“I was about to cry, but he didn’t know that,” Ariel said. “I was shittin’ bricks scared.”
LaRussell put on the first outfit—a camo two-piece set with a purple beanie and pair of white shades. Ariel waited anxiously for his thoughts.
“NIGGA!” LaRussell said, looking at himself in the mirror.
LaRussell liked it, he really, really liked it. By the end of the fitting, Ariel gained the approval of Tietta.
“You’re hired!” Tietta said.
Just like that, Ariel became the newest member of Good Compenny.
“It was really a seamless integration,” LaRussell said. “My mom and them fuck with her—it was very very seamless, it was effortless.”
Soon enough, “The Ariel Effect” began—visuals for LaRussell’s “How Could I Not” and “Cali Bear” went viral, the hype reaching back to the buzz of the golden music video era of BET’s 106 & Park and MTV’s TRL. LaRussell’s comments became flooded with people reacting, all sharing the same sentiment: “Oh, he finally got dressed!”
“I remember we had this analogy—it was like, man, not putting on a fit, when the videos be so great, it’s like putting the kitchen chair inside of a Lamborghini,” LaRussell said.
The “Hit-Boy” visuals–where LaRussell wore a knit Michael Jordan pullover with a blue sky background that complimented the cool tones in both the classic Mustang and Hitboys teal Adidas t-shirts he often paired it with—landed LaRussell in Forbes.
LaRussell recalled finding out about the Forbes article on one of the last days of his Seattle residency. It happened to be the only day of the extended trip where he woke up in a mood that he just couldn’t shake, but when he saw the article, it made him step back and think.
“It was just like a, like a reminder, like, damn, bro,” LaRussell said. “Forbes, Lil nigga from the V? Independently?!? Black!?! Forbes, no major, no - no …. Forbes!?! Man…. What we doing?”
Since their initial fitting, LaRussell and Ariel’s relationship has grown. He’s even got a nickname for her—Mei Ling, but that’s a story for another day. LaRussell never felt like Ariel was trying to force her vision on him too fast, too soon. This slow building of trust led to a point where things that LaRussell originally said “fuck no” to are now a maybe, and sometimes, without much hesitation, a yes.
“Our agreement was I’ll let you do your thing, but to my level of comfort,” LaRussell said. “I gotta look like me and I gotta feel good.”
On January 2nd, LaRussell posted on Instagram:
“I NEVER wanted a Stylist. I wanted to just feel and look like myself and I felt a stylist would compromise my brand. I just wanted to wear sweats and shorts lol. Mei Ling really came in and just allowed a nigga to flourish lol. I still wear sweats and shorts but we make it fashion! IT WASN’T EASY! But we get it done! I’m super grateful to have you on the team dawg!”
