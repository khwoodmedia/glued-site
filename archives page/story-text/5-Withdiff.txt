10. WithdifficultyWithpleasure
Sunday stood at the front of Heron Studios in Oakland, California. The room was warm and the lighting intimate. A few rows of chairs in a small room. A mic and soundboard in the front. The typical rowdy Family Not a Group crowd, is speckled with elders, chatting, waiting in anticipation for Sunday to preview her July release, WithdifficultyWithpleasure.
Sunday sat on a stool, her hair full and framing her face, her skin glowing under the low lights. A mic rested lightly in her lap as she waited for everyone to settle. The album was a culmination of five years of collected sounds and recorded pieces coming together to capture the past, present, and future of her story.
Sunday opened the listening party with her song “U Dnt Knw Me,” a slow, deliberate introduction to the soft soliloquies that shape the entire project. As she invites listeners into the most vulnerable parts of her life, she also reminds them of an uncomfortable truth: you only ever see parts of her, and that may always be the case.
Across the ten-track album, Sunday’s exquisite vocals, layered with metaphors and lyrical storytelling, illuminate her struggles in music, her process of accepting loss and love, the complications of creating art, and her desire to show people what exists beneath the bubbly persona that has lately felt more suffocating than freeing lately. As you move through the record, you are invited into emotions she usually keeps to herself, feelings that might frighten people if she expressed them in the moment, but carry less risk when transformed into music.
The album draws from R&B, soul, and neo-soul, but not without surprises. Her playful lyricism weaves in deep-cut anime references and even an entire song about menstruation, a detail you might miss unless you listen closely. But the album isn’t without its surprises. “Mustangs in Mojave” channels the carefree euphoria of jumping on your bed as a teenage girl, belting out Avril Lavigne lyrics at the top of your lungs, giving you a taste of Sunday’s rockstar persona.
For Sunday, the album is a declaration of self. It represents showing up authentically as the weird girl, the high-functioning sad girl, the anime girl, and the girl who is done tiptoeing around other people’s feelings. It is the voice of someone no longer willing to push her emotions aside to comfort the world behind her.
