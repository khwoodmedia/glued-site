1. According to Avery
How Avery went from rapping at the Pergola to landing multiple LaRussell features, creating her own hyphy anthem, launching merch, and building her own lane, all before turning ten.
In March, I arrived at Rink Studios in Sacramento, California, for LaRussell’s show. The whole team was already there: LaRussell, Tietta, Jacob, Jason, and The Yee Section. Close behind Tietta was Avery, barely reaching her hip. I followed them into the green room.
A photographer walked in, introduced herself to LaRussell, and let him know she’d be taking photos. But the moment she spotted Avery, her jaw dropped. She dropped to her knees and started bowing, like Avery was royalty.
“Is this Avery?” she exclaimed.
“Well damn, say hi to everyone else,” LaRussell laughed.
The photographer quickly greeted the rest of the room. But reactions like that had become Avery’s new normal. Bay Area legends were introducing themselves to her.
This was the Avery effect.
Later that day, when Avery hit the stage to perform “We Be Hyphy,” the crowd erupted. Adults were rapping her lyrics word for word, holding up four fingers during her verse: “Four plus four, I know I ate!” Even I had been corrected on her lyrics by young adults who knew every bar.
The song itself was lyrically packed, from jumping off cliffs with Dre, to eating at Momo’s, to shouting out the girly pops, Avery told a full hyphy anthem from her perspective. The authenticity is what makes people, young and old, slap her music.
“It’s crazy,” she said, “because I thought only kids would know my stuff. But it’s wild how adults know it, and not just know it, they enjoy it. Like, they really enjoy it too.”
Last year, at the April 2024 Pergola Show, LaRussell made his usual call to the crowd: “Who wants to sing their favorite song?” You never knew what to expect. Some folks shrank back, shaking their heads. Others had to be coaxed onto the stage, while a few jumped up without hesitation. The stage became a mix of high-pitched screams, surprise renditions, and the occasional shockingly good performance.
Then 8-year-old Avery stepped up.
Wearing a red pullover, jeans, and a headband, she confidently grabbed the mic and requested “Hyphy 2020,” a long-time favorite in her household. Her parents stood front row as Avery climbed up on a speaker, moved her shoulders, grabbed her collar and nearly broke into a thizz face.
“Wow,” she said, rocking back and forth, eyes locked on the crowd.
“Wow,” she repeated, this time with even more focus.
The look on her face said it all, she meant business.
“Make some room, I’m a whole to take in,” she rapped, and the crowd erupted.
Over the next two minutes, Avery stole the entire show. It wasn’t just that she knew the words, it was her flawless delivery, her energy, her stage presence, and her confidence that left every single person stunned. The crowd went wild.
In the days that followed, LaRussell posted a recap on Instagram. The video went viral, amassing nearly 4 million views, 238k likes, 70,000 shares, and landing Avery a live session spot on LaRussell’s Spotify. The comments flooded in: fire emojis, love, encouragement, and, of course, the occasional hater.
Since then, Avery’s role in Good Compenny has grown exponentially.
She released her latest track “We Be Hyphy,” complete with a full visualizer. She attended her first influencer event at Foot Locker during NBA All Star Weekend, performed at E.L.I.T.E. Public Schools, and has become a regular member of Good Compenny.
People have even started bringing their kids to LaRussell, hoping they’ll have the same breakout moment Avery did. But as LaRussell shared in a passing conversation,
“I never chose Avery. She was chosen.”
It was happenstance at best. Her path was carved by the opportunity meeting the moment.
Now, Avery and her parents are at nearly every Good Compenny event. Recently, she joined Tietta on stage during House Rules, always announcing Rule #3: “Have fun!”
She’s interviewing artists, Good Compenny and beyond, on her own platform. She even hit the airwaves on 94.9 to share more about her story.
And the truth is, she’s just getting started.
“Honestly, it was crazy. I didn’t think I’d be able to do this,” said Avery.
Inspired by LaRussell and supported every step of the way by her parents, who are never more than a few feet behind her, Avery has been able to explore multiple creative paths, from interviewing your favorite artists to making music of her own.
Early on, her parents recognized that Avery didn’t just want to be a rapper, and they knew better than to box her in. It was clear: she was too talented to be limited to just one thing.
Every day, Avery’s stage presence grows sharper. She doesn’t just rap, she dances, controls her voice, stays on beat, and delivers her verses with breath and power, taking the crowd along for the ride. And by her side are her fellow GC kids, not just her peers, but her biggest supporters, rapping along to her lyrics like they wrote them themselves.
Now, Avery is part of a new wave of young kids leading their own hyphy movement, one rooted in a kid-friendly way to party.
Avery is proof of what’s possible when you give young people the tools to shine. She’s showing other kids that the sky isn’t the limit, it’s just the beginning.
