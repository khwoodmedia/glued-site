4. Sunday Service
Amid smoke, a new healing sound emerged, part prayer, part protest.
In a time of immense grief, LaRussell and Sunday Service’s performance at their Grammy weekend benefit concert served as a powerful reminder of music power. Held during the first weekend of February, just a month after the Palisades fire began, the event supported those affected by the L.A. fires and offered a moment of healing for both performers and the crowd.
The concert united three creative forces: Sunday Service, the CO-LLAB Choir, and Good Compenny, coming together to blend gospel, soul, and LaRussell’s one-of-a-kind flow. The performance reimagined LaRussell’s discography and popular rap songs, like “Ballin” by Mustard ft. Roddy Ricch, by transforming their lyrics into gospel renditions.
The Good Compenny team arrived a few days before the February 7th concert. At first glance, much of L.A. looked unchanged, billboards advertising the latest Netflix series still towered over Downtown and Hollywood, palm trees cut into the clear blue skies, but there was a quiet weight in the air. Grief was present, even in places that appeared untouched.
Rehearsals took place on the 12th floor of the W Hotel, where panoramic views of Los Angeles stretched in every direction, including a perfect view of Capitol Records. At the center of the rehearsal room stood a small stage with a piano and mini organ, sheet music placed neatly on top. On the opposite side, nearly two dozen members of the Sunday Service Choir filled the rows of seats. At the center of it all was Jason White, the choir director.
Jason’s roots in music ran deep. Raised in the church, he’d been singing hymns and fusing genres since childhood. Over the years, he worked with legendary artists like Jennifer Hudson, Beyoncé, and Mary J. Blige. Now, he stood before us, clapping rhythmically, motioning with his hands, and, most of all, communicating with his eyes. His expressions were animated and full of life, occasionally prompting bursts of laughter from the choir. But with so much of the performance depending on facial expression, it was all intentional.
The choir was dressed comfortably. Some singers moved their hands with each note, raising or lowering them in sync with the melody, others waved along with the rhythm. Rehearsals were occasionally paused to clarify subtle distinctions between notes. Every song centered around themes of resistance and rebirth. Unlike the fast-paced delivery often found in rap, the gospel reworkings gave space for the lyrics to land, clear, powerful, and intentional.
The organ provided a cinematic backdrop to the choir, turning each voice into part of a larger emotional landscape. The piano shifted the mood, adding mystery, intensity, excitement, depending on its pace and rhythm. When a section hit the right note, Jason would offer a soft nod of approval. And when the harmony locked in, he’d loosen up, smiling, dancing.
He called on individual singers, clapped with his hands raised, stomped in time with the beat, and occasionally stepped over to the piano himself to play a chord or demonstrate a note. His energy was contagious. As his excitement built, the choir rose to their feet, clapping and dancing with him.
By noon, the choir broke for water, still humming melodies under their breath. LaRussell and the Good Compenny team arrived to begin full-group rehearsals. The following day, the entire crew was present: CO-LLAB Choir, the Yee Section, Sunday Service. Waiting for them were matching outfits, brown sweatsuits and socks for Good Compenny and CO-LLAB; long, cream-colored shirts for the sound crew; and cream sweatsuits for Sunday Service. The fashion coordinator explained the choice: brown for grounding, cream for healing and purity.
Early in the practice, Jason remarked, “I’m being bombarded by music.” With nearly 40 voices and a dozen instrumentalists, blending everything into one cohesive sound was no small feat. But with time, the sweet spots emerged. CO-LLAB and Sunday Service found their harmonies. The musicians carved out space for one another. A unified sound was born.
By the time the show began, every seat was filled. Guests arrived in their Sunday best.
LaRussell, Good Compenny, CO-LLAB Choir, and Sunday Service entered down two aisles to the haunting opening note of Kanye’s “Runaway.” The entrance was marked by a captivating stillness as everyone moved toward the stage in neutral tones, slowly forming a circle around the center.
Over the next hour, Sunday Service brought the crowd to church. Words fail to describe the experience. When dozens of voices meet in perfect harmony, a frequency is set in the room that reverberates beyond sound, a vibration technology still can’t replicate. It was something you had to feel.
“Runaway” was reimagined as a prayer, for the children, for the families, for the hurting. LaRussell’s own tracks, like “GC Coupe,” “Caught Up in My Head,” and “Peanut Butter Insides,” were transformed into gospel, layered with a Bay Area rhythm and style. At one point, the music faded out completely, leaving only the instrumental of “Tell Me When to Go” and “Gas Break Dip,” giving the hyphy movement it’s own moment to shine.
Tears welled up. Smiles stretched wide. Some bowed their heads in stillness, others stood with arms open in praise, singing along. The energy in the room was palpable. Every song became a sermon, every harmony a hymn. Music, once again, proved itself as a balm, for grief, for loss, for all of us. It reminded everyone in the room of the strength found in community, and the healing power of sound.
