7. Sausee: From BART to the Big Stage
“I dance my heart and soul out. And I have fun. I enjoy life, and I just love to create.”
In 2015, Sausee was in his early 20s, taking BART from Richmond to Oakland for work. After a long ride, he finally arrived, only to find out he’d been taken off the schedule. So, he got right back on the BART and headed home.
He hadn’t been particularly attached to that job, or the ones before it. Something always went wrong. Either he got fired, or something happened. On the ride back, he ran into a friend and fellow dancer on the BART who was performing and asked him, “Why don’t you just dance?”
He wasn’t working anyway, so why not make some money and have fun? It had been years since he really danced, but it had always been a part of him for as long as he could remember. Still, after high school, he’d tried to take a more traditional route: college, a steady job, the “safe” thing. But that day, something clicked.
He couldn’t think of a good reason not to dance.
Like no time had passed, the passion came back instantly. It felt easy. Natural. And better yet, there was money in it. A lightbulb went off. The 9-to-5s weren’t working, but maybe this could. Maybe this was his shot to do something he truly loved, and make it work.
So, Sausee started dancing on BART more regularly. At first, it was just to make ends meet. But the more he did it, the more his passion grew. He found himself quickly immersed in the culture he’d loved since he was a kid. On top of that, his warm inviting spirit allowed him to connect with people from all different walks of life on the BART.
That moment became even more important in 2018, when Sausee became a father. His daughter was born visually impaired.
“Once I had her, I was like, forget this. I’m gonna dance or die trying. Whatever comes with it, I don’t care.”
He didn’t care what it cost, he was going to make a name for himself dancing, no matter what.
“My daughter gonna know that she had a dad that was really out here doing something. Somebody’s gonna be able to tell her, ‘Your dad was doing this, your dad was doing that. Your dad was on TV.’”
Her birth gave him a new kind of fuel. A whole new energy to turn his dream into reality.
Sausee’s love for dance started young. Around five years old, his grandmother noticed he had an incredible sense of rhythm, better than most adults. From his earliest memories, he loved to move.
His early influences included Michael Jackson, B2K (especially Raz-B), and Chris Brown. He danced at every family function, every gathering in the neighborhood. He was a Richmond kid through and through, dancing, playing basketball, hanging out at Nicholl Park, and sometimes getting into a little trouble.
He always had it.
During community celebrations like Juneteenth, he’d perform and quickly built a reputation for his skill. People began pulling him on stage just to turn the crowd up. He remembers times as a kid when whole buildings would go wild, people standing up, clapping, yelling, captivated by his energy.
One of his most defining early memories came when his mom took him to a talent show at Richmond Community College. He was about seven years old and was his older cousins’ “secret weapon.” His family, Kool John, Jay Anthony, and Rossi, were part of a dance crew called The Main Event, before HBK became a rap group. The crew also included IAMSU, LoveRance, Young Bari, Jay Rock, and others.
His mom used to take him to their rehearsals. There was no choreography planned for him, his only job was to hit the stage and dance. When it was his time, he went out and gave it everything. The crowd, around a thousand people, went crazy.
And sitting in the front row? Missy Elliott, who had performed earlier that day.
That moment was unforgettable. It was one of the first times Sausee knew he wanted to keep dancing.
“Yeah, this is what I like. I like this. Because my love language is making people smile.”
Throughout his journey, from childhood into adulthood, he was shaped by the dancers around him: his family, the community, and the teachers who taught him how to show up on time, how to read a room, how to turn his energy up or down when needed, and how to navigate the entertainment world while staying true to himself.
Even as a kid who wore his heart on his sleeve, Sausee knew:
He was meant to dance.
This all culminated in 2020, when the pandemic hit. While most people were stuck inside scrolling on their phones, collecting unemployment, and laying low, Sausee had a plan.
He poured all his money into a cameraman and spent his days shooting five or six dance videos in different outfits, every single day. He kept it up for weeks straight.
And people noticed. His audience started growing. But more importantly, they felt what he was doing. His energy, his authenticity, it resonated.
After the pandemic, he started walking around the Bay, and it became clear just how many people had been watching his grind. Strangers would come up to him, telling him he was their favorite rapper. That’s how magnetic his presence was.
LaRussell had peeped Sausee years before. The first time was at Cornerstone, when fellow artist Poo$ie was performing. Unbeknownst to Sausee, LaRussell and Tietta were in the front row, watching closely. They exchanged numbers after the show, but nothing came of it for a few years.
Then, last year, LaRussell hit him up out of the blue and invited him to come through to The V.
Sausee was playing video games with his boys when he got the call. LaRussell asked if he could pull up for 20 minutes. He lived just five minutes away, so of course he said yes. But he didn’t show up alone, he brought his friends too.
LaRussell’s only instruction: “Get off.”
And Sausee did exactly that.
Their connection was instant. Sausee’s presence, his crew, his movement, it was all appreciated. He ended up appearing in multiple visualizers with LaRussell. So when it came time to build a performance crew for the 2025 NBA All-Star Rising Star Showcase, LaRussell didn’t hesitate, he brought in Sausee.
For Sausee, it felt like a moment straight from the universe. He had just been in talks to be part of the 2025 Kendrick Lamar Super Bowl performance, but it didn’t pan out. The very next day, LaRussell called with the All-Star opportunity.
“He called me a breath of fresh air. It felt like God was in the building. I literally wanted to cry. I’ve been working so hard, dancing for so many years, and sometimes it felt like nobody was watching. But he reminded me, we see you. We love you. And we’re gonna make sure you’re in here.”
For over a decade, Sausee has brought joy to the Bay Area, through his art, his energy, and his unwavering passion.
These days, he teaches kids how to dance Monday through Friday, while still pushing his own career forward. Every step he’s taken has been about more than just himself, it’s about giving back, inspiring the next generation, and moving through life with purpose.
