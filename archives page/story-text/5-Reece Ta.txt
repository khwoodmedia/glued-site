4. Reece Take ’Em to Church
From Oakland church pews to gold plaques, Reece traces faith, loss, and perseverance in a deeply personal chapter of his career.
The drums were Reece’s first love. Growing up in Oakland, California, he was always banging on things, playing loudly and unapologetically. The drums became the perfect outlet for that energy. Church was the place that held his earliest musical memories. Between the choir, percussion, and keys, he had space to experiment freely, surrounded by gospel music and community. That foundation later inspired his producer tag, “Reece take ’em to church,” a phrase that now precedes some of his hardest-hitting beats. His credits range from “All Me” with Bay Area legends Kehlani and Keyshia Cole to Mozzy’s “Excuse Me,” both of which earned him gold plaques.
Now signed to EMPIRE, Reece has spent years shaping the sound of artists including YG, Rexx Life Raj, 22nd Jim, P-Lo, and E-40. When he’s not producing, he is quick to jump on an instrument, his versatility allowing him to move effortlessly between roles in the studio. While he has firsthand experience working with some of the biggest names in the industry, this year marks a shift. Reece is opening up a more personal chapter with the release of his own album, created in memory of his cousin Snacks, who passed away from a fentanyl overdose in 2022.
Snacks was one of the first people to teach Reece how to program beats and play the keyboard. That bond, and that loss, sit at the heart of this project.
Church and Reece’s sound are inseparable. The emotion, the release, and the sense of connection he felt growing up all carried over into his music.
“Church is my whole field,” Reece said during an interview at EMPIRE earlier this year.
That influence shows up not just stylistically, but emotionally. He recalled a moment in the studio that captured the power music has always held for him.
“I was in the studio, and I was playing the keys when I kept hearing someone who sounded like they were crying. I kept looking back, thinking I must be tripping. The third time I looked back, I asked, ‘Are you okay?’ And she said, ‘Whatever it is that you’re playing, whatever you’re doing, it just keeps making me cry.’”
That same feeling Reece experienced throughout his life in church is what he aims to translate into every record, no matter who is on the track. His love for music has brought success, but it has never come without sacrifice. Being a producer means long nights, fourteen-hour days, and pushing through an industry that is not inherently built for people to thrive. It means showing up even when you do not want to.
“You know, I just try to stay prayed up and grounded,” Reece said. “God, a higher power, whatever you believe in, helps keep my mind steady, because this job is insane.”
This album is an invitation into Reece’s world. Not just the plaques and the hits, but the trials that exist alongside them. More than a year and a half in the making, the project is meant to be experienced, tracing pain, resilience, and survival. Even the rollout reflects that intention.
Rather than only releasing singles on streaming platforms, Reece has been revisiting the places that shaped him. He has returned to old schools to speak with teachers who once guided him. He has gone back to the two shelters where he lived at twelve and thirteen with his mom and sister, talking with kids who now occupy the same spaces. Walking through those buildings again, he was struck by how little had changed.
Grief often goes unnoticed. After memorials and funerals pass, collective mourning fades. What remains is personal, carried quietly or shared only with those closest to you. People learn how to smile, keep conversations moving, and continue living, even as the weight of loss settles deep in the heart. This project lives in that space, honoring what was lost while finding a way forward.
