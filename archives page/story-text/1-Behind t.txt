Story 6: Behind the Beats: DJ Gutta Butta Presents 3-Point Play
The album DJ Gutta Butta Presents: 3-Point Play dropped in February, leaving us with one of the most iconic Bay Area LaRussell moments, including features from Keak Da Sneak, J. Stalin, OMB Peezy, and DB.Boutabag.
Though they created the album on a short two-day trip in Las Vegas, it was latent with versatility from bars full of life lessons, punctuated by LaRussell’s personal journey to hyphy shit that Tietta and Splash rapped for weeks like it was their own, before it came out.
DJ Gutta Butta is the mastermind behind the album. LaRussell was originally in Vegas to do an appearance for Zumeiz when he posted that he needed some studio space. Gutta Butta hit LaRussell, quickly connecting him with a spot. As soon as he landed, LaRussell headed to the studio.
“I wouldn’t care if I worked with anyone else, but LaRussell, till the end of my fucking time.” - Mike G.
DJ Gutta Butta, producer Mike G, and LaRussell teamed up a few years ago for the song “All About Fetti,” with DaBoii but the trio never had the chance to work together in person until this point. The two-day recording session was filled with an energy that’s still hard to describe. Good Compenny always seemed to bring something special to the table, something that, in the making of the feature-laden album, DJ Gutta Butta and Mike G matched with their own talents.
According to DJ Gutta Butta and Mike G, several things made LaRussell’s presence in the booth different but one thing in particular stood out: LaRussell did every song in one take, no hesitation. This is hardly the norm. It’s more common for artists to repeat their verse as many times as it takes, then the producer pieces the best sections together to make it sound as if it was done fluidly in one take. LaRussell spit effortlessly, erroneously, each time.
Mike G
Michael Gorbani, a 26-year-old record producer and engineer from Las Vegas, first started producing when he was 14 after his big sister bought him his first keyboard, equipped with Ableton Live—a digital audio workstation with software to record, edit, and produce music.
A self-described garage producer, Gorbani worked out of his parents’ garage until he eventually went to college, largely at his father’s request. It was during this time that he started putting 21 Savage-type beats on YouTube. After a few months away at college, Gorbani quickly realized that he wasn’t interested and wanted to pursue music and return home instead.
In 2020, 21 Savage’s producer held a contest that Gorbani and 60 others entered. Gorbani secured fourth place and received an email list featuring several artists, including Lil Keed and Young Thug, to whom he could send beats. Winning helped Mike G realize the caliber of his beats. One of his many strengths was his versatility—he could produce anything from West Coast trap to Detroit-style, even R&B, which set him apart from other producers.
Gorbani began selling beats without realizing he was also navigating a health challenge—a tumor had been developing in his pituitary gland for nearly six years, causing depression, weight fluctuations, and other symptoms. It wasn’t until Gorbani accompanied his sister, who has Crohn’s disease, to a doctor’s appointment that he realized something was wrong.
“I remember waking up from surgery and the first thing I saw was my mom on my right-hand side,” Gorbani said. “And I told her, ‘I want to do this, like I really want to push and just go for it and make beats.’”
As soon as Gorbani left the hospital, he was eager to start making beats again. Soon after, he got invited to his first studio session where he met a producer who’d help him get better doing what he loved and lived for.
Now, Gorbani’s has made music with LaRussell, Slimmy B, SOB, MBML and several others.
“He’s exceptional, ” LaRussell said, when asked about Gorbani and his love of producing. “He loves it. He loves it and he’s excited and enthusiastic about it. And he doesn’t complain…he’s hungry. He knows what he wants and he goes after it.”
Gorbani loves it so much that shortly after the 3-Point Play dropped, the Vegas-born producer moved to Vallejo.
DJ Gutta Butta
DJ Gutta Butta is a Bay Area legend hailing from Vallejo, CA, who now works in Las Vegas. A self-described connector, DJ Gutta Butta can be found anywhere from DJing to hosting events, producing singles and EPs of his own, to putting on concerts for artists like 310Babii, Stunna Girl and others.
“A great connector is a man who just loves to see everybody win,” Gutta said.
Gutta is the common thread between LaRussell and Mike G, making plays behind the scenes.
“Gutta Butta, I love, because he never came to me on no favor shit,” said LaRussell.
LaRussell went on to explain that everytime he connected with Gutta, Gutta came with a budget and added value.
“He is always of service and he’s always trying to pour into the pie,” LaRussell said. “It ain’t never on no other energy so it was dope to just cultivate from there.”
Gutta knows that LaRussell is particular when it comes to his music. There were several verses from talented artists that didn’t make the cut for the album.
“There was nothing wrong with the verse–he just wanted more energy,” said Gutta.
Growing up, it was The Temptations that first made Gutta want to do music. In high school, Gutta Butta described himself as outgoing, an athlete and a hustler, always making plays, like he still does today. He first got his start working in the music scene with his high school group the LRG, Little Rap Group, who he’d help throw parties across the Bay Area.
