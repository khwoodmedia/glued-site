Story 6: Grill Studios
Hip-Hop in May as LaRussell Led a Legendary Collaboration
Throughout May, LaRussell embarked on a venture to bring together a range of artists at various stages in their careers to create something special at Grill Studios in Emeryville. He reached out to the owner, pitched the idea, and offered royalties on all the resulting music. The owner happily agreed.
“It’s really dope because we did so much collaborative stuff in there that he gets to be a benefactor of all that taking place in the studio. It just continues the legacy of the Grill,” said LaRussell.
The studio sessions resulted in four mini albums, featuring artists like Michael Sneed, Nef the Pharaoh, 03 Greedo, Hey Kujo, MoneyHillCasino, Tietta and more. Producers and engineers included Mike G, CallMeClayDough, MadeByHarry, QuakeBeatz, and Link+Up, and was accompanied by instrumentalists Michael Prince and Hokage Simon.
“It’s not easy to work with us,” said LaRussell, sitting in his Vallejo apartment. “You gotta do it on the spot there. And people aren’t used to that, really. Richie Rich was like, one of my favorites. Watching him write in real-time and work was just a special moment. But everybody really showed up and balled.”
Michael Sneed was among the artists called upon to do quick turnaround verses.
“He’s like, ‘Yeah, we do this thing where we write verses in 15 minutes.’ So I did a 10/15-minute verse and wrote it real quick,” said Michael Sneed. “I ain’t gonna lie, the beat was pretty hard to write to, so I probably got maybe four or eight bars by the time it was time for me to record. So I wrote the eight bars and then had to freestyle the rest of the verse.”
What came from it was a range of four mini albums, showcasing the full spectrum of hip-hop, including storytelling and life lessons like “Goin Places” featuring MoneyHillCasino and Tietta, produced and engineered by QuakeBeats and Link+Up, to Cali Classics that make you want to roll the windows down with 03 Greedo. Oakland producer Link+Up, who has worked with LaRussell on projects like “Motion,” “The Fresh Prince of the V,” “Outstanding,” and more, reflected on the chemistry they’ve built over the years.
“Energy is unmet,” said Link+Up. “I haven’t met an artist like him yet, but it’s always good vibes when he’s in the booth. He kind of just blacks out, does his thing, and nobody can tell him any different.
He knows what he loves, and I love that I can be creative with him, and he doesn’t hold me into a box. He just kind of lets me be. And I feel like that’s how we end up coming up with the crazy stuff,” said Link+Up.
Also among the all-star roster was San Francisco native and producer QuakeBeatz, who grew up with an equal appreciation for Motown and Smokey Robinson, as well as the hyphy movement. While QuakeBeatz didn’t produce a song, he did engineer several.
“It’s just like his culture. I feel like what he stands for, the positivity. Always giving somebody a message within the music. I feel good to be a part of that,” said QuakeBeatz.
The album served as a cultural marker, showcasing what hip-hop unity looks like and, more importantly, what it sounds like.
