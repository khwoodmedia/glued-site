5. 12 Tips for Small Artists Building a Big Name
By Lazā
Lazā is a Seattle-based artist whose love for performance began in church, where she grew up surrounded by prayer, hymns, and a congregation that doubled as her first audience. Raised by pastor parents, she was introduced to music early through gospel legends like The Sheri Sisters, Deitrick Haddon, and Marvin Sapp, with her mother writing raps for her to perform during church plays. That foundation shifted in middle school when her brother handed her an iPod Nano, opening her world to Drake, T-Pain, and Cash Money and solidifying her desire to rap.
By her early teens, Lazā was performing at talent shows and by fifteen she played her first ticketed show, secretly pursuing music despite her parents’ uncertainty. Refusing college and committing fully to her craft, she began releasing music independently, dropping her first single “Zoom” in 2019 and steadily building her sound and team in Seattle. A major turning point came in 2023 when her EP All In One Night gained traction on TikTok, dramatically increasing her streams and marking a new level of confidence in her sound.
Her momentum has continued, with 2025 becoming her most-streamed year yet and a performance at Capitol Hill Block Party signaling her growing presence. Now learning to produce her own music and releasing consistently, Lazā is focused on expanding her reach, collaborating with new producers, and building her career brick by brick, shaping her vision in real time rather than waiting for permission.
12 Tips
1. Build a small, committed team. Find people who genuinely believe in you. This might include a producer, engineer, videographer, or friends who support your vision.
2. Work with collaborators who are willing to grow with you. Look for producers who will create original music for you and videographers who are willing to pull up and shoot because they believe in your potential.
3. Create a plan for your artistic output. Decide what consistent action looks like for you, such as releasing a song every month.
4. Execute the plan with consistency. Put up shots even when you’re unsure how things will turn out. Consistency builds momentum.
5. Use the resources you have. If you cannot pay for shoots, set up your phone. If you do not have a producer, use accessible tools like BandLab to experiment and begin creating.
6. Use YouTube beats strategically (but do not rely on them long-term). You can start practicing or writing over YouTube beats, then work with producers to recreate the vibe or BPM in an original way.
7. Take advantage of affordable options. There are producers selling beats for as low as $25. Getting started does not require a lot of money; it requires initiative.
8. Leverage TikTok and short-form content. Posting daily 30-second videos can introduce your music to new listeners. Many artists are discovered this way now.
9. Collaborate horizontally. You do not need to reach out to the biggest artist or producer. Look for peers in your city or online who are building at the same pace you are.
10. Document your process across platforms. Share your music on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and any space where it can find an audience.
11. Keep improving the quality of your music. Consistency will help you get better. Over time, your craft will catch up to your ambition.
12. Trust the brick-by-brick approach. Build slowly: a team, a plan, steady releases, daily content, and ongoing practice. If you stay consistent, the work will eventually take hold.
