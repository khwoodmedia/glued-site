9. Empress Theatre
Throughout January and February, LaRussell hosted The Empress Theatre Residency, bringing hundreds of people to Vallejo to celebrate music, comedy, and the Bay. The Empress Theatre, with its red and gold interior and towering ceilings adorned with sculpted swirls, has long held a special place in LaRussell’s heart. One of his first major shows was thrown there, and he’s returned every year since. In October 2022, he celebrated his birthday at the Empress, bringing out artists like DaBoii and Mistah F.A.B. Now, he’s returned once again with a fresh lineup of special guests.
Every time I walked into the Empress, it felt familiar, but in true LaRussell fashion, he continues to expand the boundaries of his craft. This time, alongside the usual band setup, there was a harp and a full choir in rehearsal. The Co-LLAB Choir, an Oakland-based collective led by Cava (who is also music teacher in Oakland), joined the ensemble for a three-show run, with nearly a dozen singers rotating in and out.
The addition of the choir and harp brought another layer to LaRussell’s ever-evolving sound. The harp was soft, at times barely audible, but when its notes floated through the air, they were unforgettable. The choir was ethereal. Their voices elevated every moment, from offering ad-libs between verses to taking over the chorus entirely. It was an experience that lingered, pushing the audience’s expectations of what hip-hop performances could be.
Each show had its own standout moments.
Twista took the stage and performed “Overnight Celebrity,” with the Co-LLAB Choir singing the signature vocals. Their voices rose and fell like angels weaving through Twista’s rapid-fire delivery, it was a beautiful collision of grit and grace.
Comedian and actor Ron Funches flew in from Los Angeles to perform short comedy skits between songs. His humor was topical, slightly self-deprecating, and effortlessly lighthearted. He had the crowd in dying laughing, riffing on the struggle of resisting CRUMBL cookies, the hardships faced by actors during wildfire season, and even poking fun at LaRussell’s line “1.5 off rappin,” with a playful, “Hey, you’ll get there one day.”
Rexx Life Raj also blessed the stage, performing some of LaRussell’s favorite songs. His set was raw and introspective, each lyric offering a glimpse into his life. The vulnerability in his performance felt deeply personal, like the kind of music that had once walked LaRussell through pivotal chapters of his own journey.
Bay Area legends B-Legit and Spice-1 closed out a night with the classic “City to City.” The moment was unforgettable, a reminder that LaRussell isn’t just building something new, but honoring what came before him. He curated a show that paid homage to hip hop’s roots, spotlighted emerging talent, and still managed to shine in his own right.
The audience got to witness Avery perform “We Be Hyphy,” Tietta share a powerful piece of spoken word, and Diva show off her dancing skills. Throughout the night, there were countless moments where kids and other community members joined LaRussell on stage to speak about their experiences being part of Good Compenny. Malachi also took the mic, continuing to wow the crowd with his soulful vocals.
With each show, the collective grew stronger. The Co-LLAB Choir became more confident, finding their rhythm between songs and learning more of LaRussell’s music week by week. Geo quickly became woven into the fabric of Good Compenny, and alongside her, artists like Simon and Clay found creative ways to bring the harp into focus, letting its subtle beauty shine through the larger sound of the group.
There was something special about coming back to the Empress and seeing the same faces in the crowd, alongside new ones drawn in by the magic. Although this may be the last show at the venue (due to differences with management), the Empress will forever be a snapshot of LaRussell’s rise and the community that rose with him.
