Story 4: AdventureIsDre: Healing, It’s Different
In 2019, Dre took a trip to the Sutro Baths outlook in San Francisco. The waves crashed on what used to be an ocean pool aquarium back in 1894 and left jagged rocks behind. As soon as he got there, he got déjà vu and started wondering why it all seemed familiar.
“The minute I saw the outlook, every memory I had there came back. To the point where I could remember the sandwich saran wrap. How my stepmom folded the sandwiches up,” said Dre.
He hadn’t thought about the Sutro Baths, or the cliffs below, for over 15 years, but something called him to that moment.
It reminded him of his father, who was born in 1947 and loved nature. Dre’s dad used to take him to places like the Sutro Baths lookout and Yosemite.
“I saw snow, rain, hail, sunshine, all in one day, and it blew me away because I didn’t even know you could have all of that,” said Dre.
Dre and his family drove around the national park, and he witnessed feeding coyotes and wildlife he had never seen before. He remembered begging his dad and uncle to stay just one more day, but it wasn’t an option. He returned home solemn.
Dre first heard LaRussell in 2020 when he was living in Georgia. George Floyd had recently been murdered, and someone had reposted a video of LaRussell’s song “20 Shots,” which he released in 2018 under his previous name, Tota Shakur.
Dre was shocked that someone from his hometown was rapping like this.
“I was just like, damn, there’s nobody from Vallejo speaking on something like this, like, unheard of,” said Dre.
Dre described himself as old school, someone who grew up on lyricism and the other fundamental pillars of hip-hop. Music wasn’t hitting the same as it was before, but LaRussell’s sound was different.
2020 was also a time of immense grief for Dre. He had lost everyone close to him—his mother, his father and his grandmother - who he cared for up until the moment she passed. When his dad died in 2020, he had never felt so lost. There was no one to talk to, no one to get advice from.
“I used to weigh like 450 pounds. I was depressed, like I was a recluse. I didn’t go outside,” said Dre.
Dre dismissed his friends’ pleas to leave the house. He could never come up with a good reason to step outside.
One night, feeling overwhelmed, Dre decided he was going to crash his car while drinking. First, he reached out to a friend with whom he had a strained relationship, but there was no answer. Determined, he got into his car and turned the key, but it wouldn’t start. He tried again and again, but the engine remained unresponsive. A few minutes later, his phone buzzed—it was his friend returning his call.
“I was like, was that meant to happen?” Dre couldn’t believe it, but he took it as a sign.
He thought to himself, “since I didn’t go through with it, maybe I can get out of this and somehow change it. So eventually I did. I started turning things around, like weight loss and trying to get my mental right.”
Dre got into therapy at one point, but it was nature that brought him the most peace.
Rediscovering Nature
Dre described nature as something that happened upon him, rather than something intentional, but once he became committed and enthusiastic about discovering what it had to offer, he couldn’t stop. He hadn’t been outside in nature for years, yet there were many familiarities from his childhood. The urge to go find something, something that made him feel at peace, in awe, challenged, overwhelmed — a waterfall, an unexpectedly beautiful trail, a stunning look out.
In 2021, Dre moved from Georgia, back to California, with a purpose. He wanted to keep the weight off, continue to lose weight, and eventually start a hiking group. He treated hiking like a second job. He worked Monday through Friday taking pictures of cars for dealerships, and on Saturdays and Sundays, he would scope out trails, find waterfalls, and locate breathtaking spots across California. Saturdays and Sundays soon turned into Tuesdays and Thursdays after work and by the time he wanted to start his hiking group, it was easy. He had already built a consistent routine at that point, and now the job was just introducing other people to it. Still, he reflected on the days when just started hiking again.
“I was 300 pounds doing this shit — like this is hard.”
Now, when team members are winded, drained, and completely unmotivated they sometimes yelled, “We’re not you!” to Dre, to which he would respond:
“You could be me though! I was where you are right now.”
“I always tell people, do shit when you don’t want to do it, because that’s the best time to do it. You’ll be breaking down the bad habits.””
After watching Good Compenny from across the country and then moving back to California, Dre continued to follow Good Compenny but never managed to grab a ticket. When Shanese reached out to invite him, Dre insisted, again, that he didn’t have a ticket, but Shanese assured him he was welcome to come. He attended the June Pergola show in 2023 and was amazed, becoming an even bigger fan than before.
Since then, Dre has become a familiar face at Good Compenny events, known for his impressive edits, stunning hikes, and vibrant demeanor. He’s been seen editing videos for other artists, working with Manny at Momo’s, jet skiing with Coach Ott and his sons, and, of course, attending the next Good Compenny show.
Dre’s story serves as an inspiration for others finding ways to stay active and support their mental health. Still, he occasionally reflects on a different version of himself, one that struggled to leave the house at all.
“I don’t know how. Eventually, I just stuck with it. That’s all it is. I stuck with the goal. I knew eventually, if I found my purpose in life, I would be able to really grab it, hold on to it, and make it what I wanted to, and that’s ultimately what I did.”
